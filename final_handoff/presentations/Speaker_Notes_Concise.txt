Slide 3 - The Data Set
- Core file: `master_customer_behavior_clean.csv`.
- Main analysis uses CFPB records.
- CFPB rows are useful because they include product, company, issue type, action, and complaint text.
- Frame the data as complaint-channel intelligence.

Slide 4 - About the Data
- Data combines customer behavior records from multiple sources.
- CFPB is the core source for structured complaint analysis.
- Key fields: company, product/service, issue, complaint text, sentiment, customer action.
- Important limitation: this is not a general population survey.

Slide 5 - Shape and Size
- Total rows: 777,867.
- Total columns: 17.
- CFPB core subset: 755,671 rows.
- CFPB time concentration: 2014 to 2017.
- PayPal/Venmo proxy cohort: 3,655 rows.
- Square/Block proxy cohort: 687 rows.
- Large-bank baseline: 167,474 rows.
- Point to make: large enough for patterns, but still complaint-channel specific.

Slide 6 - Missing Values
- URL missing: 2,702 rows.
- Issue missing: 22,196 rows, mainly non-CFPB rows.
- Country missing: 26,937 rows.
- URL and country are not used as model features.
- CFPB rows used in the core analysis are complete on issue.
- Point to make: missing values do not block the main analysis.

Slide 7 - Driving Question
- Transition slide.
- Say: now that we know the data, the next step is the research question.
- The project is not only about counting complaints.
- It is about using complaint patterns to guide product and strategy decisions.

Slide 8 - Driving Question Text
- Main question: how do CFPB complaint patterns signal escalation risk?
- Inputs considered: issue type, product line, company, customer action.
- Strategic output: which startup and enterprise opportunities does the signal point to?
- Point to make: analytics should connect to action.

Slide 9 - How Does Our Analysis Answer It?
- Descriptive: mapped complaint volume, escalation rates, and issue patterns.
- Predictive: built logistic regression using complaint text and product type.
- Prescriptive strategic: translated signal clusters into product recommendations.
- Prescriptive operational: identified market gaps and future product opportunities.
- Point to make: each layer builds on the previous layer.

Slide 10 - Data Cleaning

- Cleaning made the dataset consistent for analysis, modeling, and dashboard use.
- Goal: preserve useful rows while removing unreliable required-field gaps.

Slide 11 - Step-by-Step Process
- Dropped the rating column.

Slide 12 - Data Analysis: Five Questions
- Transition to findings.
- The five questions move from descriptive patterns to predictive signal.
- End goal: decide whether the signal is strong enough for prioritization.

Slide 13 - Question 1: Product and Issue Volume
- Method: count complaints by product type and issue category.
- Scope: CFPB-only subset.
- Highest-volume products: mortgage, debt collection, bank account/service, credit reporting.
- Dominant issues: billing disputes, debts not owed, servicing failures.
- Point to make: complaint volume is concentrated, not evenly spread.

Slide 14 - Visual Evidence for Question 1
- Use this slide to reinforce concentration.
- A few product/issue combinations carry a large share of complaint burden.
- Product teams should prioritize high-volume friction zones first.
- Link to recommendations later: billing clarity, dispute handling, lifecycle guardrails.

Slide 15 - Question 2: Formal Complaint Distribution
- Method: count customer action types and formal complaint share.
- Formal complaints: about 97.8%.
- Other outcomes: about 2.2%.
- Point to make: CFPB data is already escalation-heavy.
- Caveat: not a general satisfaction sample.
- Use as risk intelligence, not broad sentiment.

Slide 16 - Question 3: Major vs Smaller Players
- Method: compare company complaint profiles after minimum-row filtering.
- Major players: higher complaint counts and more stable patterns.
- Smaller/fintech cohorts: noisier but strategically useful.
- PayPal/Venmo: payment, fraud/scam, transaction, funds-availability friction.
- Square/Block-side: debt collection, verification, communication tactics.
- Point to make: fintech pain differs from large-bank pain.

Slide 17 - Visual Evidence for Question 3
- Contrast large banks vs fintech proxy cohorts.
- Large banks: more mortgage, servicing, and account-heavy issues.
- Fintech: payment reliability, fraud handling, transaction clarity, collections transparency.
- Point to make: do not use a one-size-fits-all financial-services strategy.

Slide 18 - Question 4: Predicting Escalation
- Model: logistic regression classifier.
- Inputs: complaint text and product line.
- Training years: 2014-2015.
- Test years: 2016-2017.
- Key signal terms: billing, fee, delinquent, disputes, APR.
- Text carries more weight than product type alone.
- Fraud/scam cases likely need a separate workflow.

Slide 19 - Question 5: Signal Stability
- Five validation checks were used.
- Tested future-year performance.
- Tested unseen product performance.
- Checked impact of removing input types.
- Checked calibration of confidence scores.
- Checked sensitivity to generic complaint phrases.
- All five checks passed.
- Calibration error: 0.0018, below 0.08 threshold.
- Point to make: stable enough for decision support.

Slide 20 - Escalation Prediction Model
- Transition to model details.
- The model helps prioritize likely escalation risk.
- It is not meant to replace human judgment.
- Use it as a product and operations signal.

Slide 21 - About the Model
- Converts complaint text into numeric features.
- Learns which words and products are associated with escalation.
- Outputs probability from 0 to 1.
- Strengths: interpretable, fast, scalable.
- Limitation: based on wording patterns, not deep reasoning.
- Best use: early warning and prioritization.

Slide 22 - Results
- Temporal ROC AUC: 1.0.
- Average precision: 1.0.
- Product-only ROC AUC: about 0.918.
- Interpretation: text adds major predictive value beyond product category.
- Caveat: near-perfect performance is dataset-specific.
- Say clearly: strong separability, not causal proof.

Slide 23 - Strategic Recommendations
- Transition from findings to action.
- Recommendations come from the complaint themes and fintech cohort patterns.
- Goal: identify what fintech PMs should build next.
- Separate fast product fixes from longer-term strategic opportunities.

Slide 24 - What Fintech PMs Should Build Next
- Payment Assurance Layer:
  transfer status trace, expected availability timer, exception alerts.
- Fraud/Scam Triage Workflow:
  guided intake, severity routing, freeze/claim workflow, SLA tracking.
- Collections Transparency Pack:
  debt verification packet, communication controls, audit trail.
- Account Lifecycle Guardrails:
  opening, closing, payoff, and state-transition clarity.
- Point to make: each recommendation maps to observed complaint patterns.

Slide 25 - Conclusion
- CFPB complaint data can work as market-intelligence signal.
- Complaint patterns reveal where escalation risk concentrates.
- Model features help translate raw text into product themes.
- Recommendations should be validated with live customer research and business metrics.
- Main caveat: complaint-channel evidence, not full-market proof.

Slide 26 - Learnings
- Complaints are structured signals, not just negative feedback.
- Strong model results matter most when translated into decisions.
- Key themes: billing clarity, dispute friction, lifecycle breaks, fraud/scam handling, expectation gaps.
- Project learning: analytics becomes more valuable when it connects to product strategy.
