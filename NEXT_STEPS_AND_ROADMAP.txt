=======================================================

Use this file as the hook for presentations and future work: predictive and
prescriptive steps should tie back to the “money” bullets below, not generic ML.


COMMITTED SCOPE — FINANCE / FINTECH MI
--------------------------------------
**Primary story:** marketing intelligence in **consumer finance / fintech** using **CFPB only**
(filter **Source = CFPB** in the dashboard and in written analysis). Same schema, same `issue`
field, same meaning of a row (public complaint channel).

**Why drop other sources for the core path:** App Store / Trustpilot / Trends rows are a
different population—often **no `issue`**, different event types, and misleading if merged into
CFPB trend lines.

**Recency note:** This extract’s CFPB bulk is **2014–2017**. That is older than a “last 3 years”
AI-paper norm, but it is **internally consistent**; say in the report that **consistency and
comparability** were chosen over a recent multi-source mix. Refreshing with newer CFPB export
is the path to both recency and a single animal.

**Appendix / optional:** Other sources in the CSV can support a **separate** question (e.g. app
reviews), not the same headline metrics as CFPB.


WHERE THE MONEY IS (IN THIS ANALYSIS) — PRECISE VERSION
-------------------------------------------------------
“Money” here means **venture- and strategy-relevant problem surface area** in
**consumer financial services**, grounded in **public complaint voice** (mainly
**CFPB**, structured **`issue` × `product_service`**), **not** proven revenue or
a census of satisfaction.

**Industry in this file** = patterns **across products and firms in that complaint
channel**, not every institution or product in the economy. Say that once in the
deck; the insight is still **actionable** for positioning and product focus.

1. Dominant (product × issue) = where to aim a differentiated offer
   The descriptive notebook’s **Demand signals** and **Complaint drivers** sections
   rank **which issues concentrate under which product lines** and how **high-
   salience vs tail firms** (by row count in *this* extract) differ in mix.
   That is the hook for a **board / management pitch**: anchor the idea in
   **repeated, named failure modes** (e.g. servicing, accuracy, collections) rather
   than generic “AI for finance.” **Tail-firm and high-salience-firm pain are both
   valid** inputs to ideation unless your thesis is specifically about serving only
   the largest incumbents’ customers.

2. Behavior and escalation (`customer_action`), not NLP sentiment
   **Formal complaint share** and **customer_action** describe **what happened**
   (escalation, churn-style actions, etc.). Supplier **sentiment** labels are
   omitted from headline notebook MI because they misread short boilerplate text.
   Risk and ops framing should use **issue, product, and action**, not “positive
   vs negative” NLP scores.

3. Operational and reputation risk (downstream of the same clusters)
   High volume on specific **issue × product** combinations flags where **service
   cost, remediation, regulatory attention, and press** tend to concentrate—
   useful for **incumbents** and for **startups** selling into pain that incumbents
   under-serve.

4. Competitive salience in the file
   **Share of voice** and **firm-tier contrasts** show **who dominates this channel**
   in the extract and **which issues attach to whom**. That is **competitive
   context**, not proof of market share; external size overlays are **optional**
   unless you need balance-sheet or segment claims.

5. Method and governance value
   One cleaning definition (`shared_cleaning_rules`), reproducible **clean CSV**,
   and a **dashboard** tied to **customer_action** and formal-complaint metrics
   make claims **traceable** and **refreshable**.

6. What this is NOT
   Not demographic segmentation (no reliable person-level demographics here).
   Not direct revenue or churn prediction without extra data and labels.
   Not a random sample of the market—always qualify **CFPB-heavy, complaint-
   conditional** lens.
   Not “population sentiment”—the dataset is **selective by channel and behavior**.

WHAT’S LEFT — PRESCRIPTIVE (ACTIONS TIED TO INSIGHTS)
------------------------------------------------------
Prescriptive here means: given MI outputs, what do we DO—even if v1 is process.

• Monitoring cadence: weekly/monthly review of top issues by product line.
• Competitor watchlist: fixed list of firms + same slices (issue mix, trend).
• Messaging / positioning tests: hypotheses from issue themes (e.g. accuracy,
  communication clarity)—run only with product truth and compliance guardrails.
• Internal owners: who refreshes the clean CSV, who approves external claims.


LIMITATIONS (ALWAYS STATE IN REPORTS)
---------------------------------------
• Complaint-heavy; not population-representative.
• Source mix (CFPB vs app stores, etc.) changes interpretation—**core MI uses CFPB-only**;
  if you show other sources, label a **different** question.
• Supplier NLP sentiment is weak on short CFPB text; core MI uses **issue**,
  **product_service**, and **customer_action**—not sentiment as an outcome.
• Events are not customers; use **issue × product** and **firm salience** tiers
  for substantive splits; optional external firm-size overlay only if you need
  institutional (not file-volume) claims.
